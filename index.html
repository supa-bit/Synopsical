What's in the drawer

Not a folder of notes — a catalogue of them.

Cross-referenced, like a real reference work
Link any entry to any other — or type [[Entry Title]] inline while you write. Every page shows what it connects to, what connects back, and how the whole compendium fits together on an interactive link map.
Finds connections you didn't make
Related entries surface on their own — first by shared tags, categories, and inline mentions, then by meaning once you've written enough for it to notice on its own.
Catalogued, not just saved
Categories, tags, structured custom fields, and sources — organized the way an actual archive is kept, not the way a folder of loose files accumulates.
Yours to look at — and yours to keep
Pick from eight themes, upload your own fonts, give each entry its own faceplate, and export or restore your entire compendium in JSON or Markdown whenever you want.
Who keeps a compendium

Built for people who keep track of things worth keeping.

01 · Research & Study
Independent researchers, students & autodidacts

Turn scattered reading notes into a permanent encyclopedia of historical periods, scientific mechanisms, papers, and primary sources — complete with dates, citations, and cross-references that hold up years later.

02 · Worldbuilding & Lore
Fiction writers, game designers & tabletop GMs

Build a living lore bible for characters, factions, locations, magic systems, and timelines. Give every entry an emblem faceplate, structured attributes, and trace every alliance or bloodline on the Link map.

03 · Personal Archives
Collectors, synthesisers & lifelong learners

Catalogue botanical specimens, architecture, film and book syntheses, or culinary references in a quiet reading room styled in your own typography — with 1-click AI-formatted bulk import and portable export.
